EDB-Orgel takes the workflow of a drawbar organ and fills it with digital synthesis. Nine drawbars each set the level of one harmonic, and each drawbar can be given its own sound source: 64 (the MOS Technology 6581 SID chip, as used in the Commodore 64 and the Elektron SidStation), PD (Casio phase distortion), FM (frequency modulation of the Yamaha DX kind) or LA (Roland linear arithmetic).

Around the drawbars sit a per-drawbar ADSR envelope, tremolo with speed and depth, per-drawbar velocity sensitivity, and vibrato on the modulation wheel. The filters are impulse responses of the real 6581 filter. Lowpass, bandpass, highpass and every combination of the three. The output is either the direct line signal or a tiny computer speaker miked with a Shure SM57, in mono or through the stereo widener.

The controls are split across a mixer tab and a modulation tab, with a dialog for loading the supplied patches.

532 samples and 8 filter impulse responses at 48 kHz / 24-bit, embedded in the plugin as lossless FLAC.

## Features

- Nine drawbars, each with its own independently chosen sound source.
- Four synthesis types: SID (MOS Technology 6581), phase distortion, frequency modulation and linear arithmetic.
- Per-drawbar ADSR envelope and per-drawbar velocity sensitivity.
- Tremolo with speed and depth, vibrato on the modulation wheel.
- Eight filter settings taken from impulse responses of the real 6581 filter: LPF, BPF, HPF and every combination.
- Direct line output or a miked computer speaker, in mono or stereo.
- Mixer tab, modulation tab and a patch dialog.
- 532 samples at 48 kHz / 24-bit, embedded losslessly (975 MB source library).
